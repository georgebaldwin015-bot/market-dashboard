Strategy review

How Edge Lab works, what the evidence says, and what your own trades say. Written 25 Sep 2026.
In one paragraph: Trade only A+ to B setups, enter on the buy-stop, put the stop at 1× ADR, and let the 4× ADR band take you out. In a bear market, trade A+/A only at half size. Across 2022–26 that system made money in every test, including the years it wasn't tuned on. Your own year was about flat (+1%) not because the entries were wrong, but because good trades were sold early and C-grade trades were taken anyway. On the same entries, the 4× exit would have made about +12%.

1 · What happens every night (UK time is UTC+1 in summer)

  • 21:30 UTC (after the US close): the screener scans ~520 US stocks, finds the ones armed for tomorrow, grades them A+ to F and publishes the ‘Today’ tab, the Market tab and the email.
  • 22:00 UTC: IBKR sync: your fills, open positions and IBKR's own daily return come in; stops are worked out for every ADR band.
  • 22:45 UTC: the forward test checks the live track record, and every one of your trades is graded the way ‘Today’ would have graded it.
  • 23:10 UTC: a second IBKR sync picks up those grades and sends the stop email: tomorrow's 4× stops, what moved, what was hit, what's off plan. Nothing is ever sent to IBKR.

2 · The setups

  • Two strategies: Minervini (the trend template: a stock in a confirmed Stage 2 uptrend above its rising averages) and QM (Qullamaggie-style momentum: a strong mover with high relative strength and a fast ADR). A stock can qualify for both.
  • Entry: a buy-stop at the 20-day highest close. It only fills if the stock opens below it and trades up through it; a gap straight over is no trade.
  • Stop: 1× ADR below the buy-stop. That distance is "1R".
  • Size: shares = 1% of the account ÷ (buy-stop − stop), halved in a bear market.

No: wider exits on the top grades tie 4× at best and collapse at 8×. Tailoring the exit by grade, strategy or market cycle also looked great on the years it was picked on and then did worse than one rule on new years. One exit, 4×, for everything.

6 · What your own trades say

  • Last year: IBKR +1.0%. The same entries and share counts on the 4× exit: +12.0%. The exit alone was worth about 11 points.
  • A+ trades: the entries were right; the exits weren't. 8 trades: −4.2R as sold, +11.0R on the 4× exit.
  • C trades: 12 trades, 1 winner, −11.7R, and no exit rescues them (−12.0R on 4×). These are the trades to stop taking.
  • Open positions are about +8.6R, so closed plus open is roughly breakeven, which matches IBKR being about flat.
Your trades are a small sample (39), so they point at habits, not at a better rule. The rules come from the 7,469-trade backtest.

7 · The checklist

  1. Only A+ to B, or A+/A in a bear market. The ‘Today’ tab greys out the rest.
  2. Enter on the buy-stop, not before and not chasing more than about 1% above.
  3. Stop at 1× ADR. Size so that stop is 1% of the account (½% in a bear).
  4. Don't sell a winner by feel. Move the stop up to the 4× band each night (the stop email tells you the level) and let it take you out.
  5. Check the Discipline panel in Account weekly: grade, entry and exit should all be close to 100%.

8 · Caveats

  • About a quarter of backtest entry days are "ambiguous": the daily bar can't say whether the dip under the stop came before or after the fill. Counting all of them as losses lowers every result but doesn't change the ranking of the exits.
  • The backtest is 2022–26: one bear market, one strong bull, and a choppy 2025–26. A different regime could shift the numbers.
  • Results use 1% risk and a cash limit on a $10k account; a much larger or margin account would behave differently.
