Strategy review

How Edge Lab works and what the evidence says. Rewritten 5 Oct 2026 on the honest backtest: a stock only counts while it was really in the S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100.
In one paragraph: Take A+ and A setups (3+ of the six points below) — but A+ only while recent setups are failing — risk 0.75% a trade, enter on the buy-stop, stop at 1× ADR, and let the Auto exit take you out. Tested honestly over 2017–26, that made about 26% a year with a worst drop of about 19%, against 15% a year and a 34% drop for simply holding the S&P 500. It comes in bursts, strong in 2020, 2023 and 2025–26. The biggest gain left on the table is your exits: selling before the rule.

1 · Why the numbers changed

  • The old backtest had hindsight. It tested today's S&P 500 / Nasdaq-100 list back to 2017, so it traded stocks like AppLovin, Lumentum and Super Micro before they joined the index (often because they had soared), when the scanner couldn't have seen them. 72% of the old profit came from those trades.
  • Now: every stock that was in either index since 2016 (770, including ~250 that have since dropped out) is tested, each only while it was a member. Stocks since delisted or taken over (Abiomed, Marathon Oil, Hess) have no price history and are the one gap left.
  • Two over-harsh assumptions were fixed: fill days too old to check on hourly bars count as surviving at the measured rate (77%) instead of all as losses, and open trades count at their latest price.
  • Every result is shown in both halves (2017–22 and 2023–26) and, for accounts, with bad / typical / good luck in which same-day setups got taken (60 shuffles). A rule only counts if it holds in both halves.

2 · What happens every night (UK time is UTC+1 in summer)

  • 21:30 UTC (after the US close): the screener scans ~520 US stocks, finds the ones armed for tomorrow, grades them and publishes the ‘Today’ tab, the Market tab and the email.
  • 22:00 UTC: IBKR sync: fills, open positions and IBKR's daily return; stops for every ADR band and the Auto exit.
  • 22:45 UTC: the forward test updates the live record and grades each of your trades the way ‘Today’ would have.
  • 23:10 UTC: the stop email: tomorrow's Auto stops, what moved, what was hit, what's off plan. Nothing is ever sent to IBKR.

3 · The setups

  • Two strategies: Minervini (trend template: Stage 2 uptrend above rising averages) and QM (Qullamaggie-style momentum: ADR 3%+, RS 80+, above the 10/20-day EMA).
  • Entry: a buy-stop at the 20-day highest close. It only fills if the stock opens below it and trades up through it; a gap straight over is no trade.
  • Stop: 1× ADR below the buy-stop. That distance is "1R".
  • Size: shares = 0.75% of the account ÷ (buy-stop − stop). With a 1× ADR stop that ties up 15–25% of a cash account per position, so about 6 at a time. At 1% you could hold only 4–5 and missed more of the rare big runners: 0.75% did better in every period tested, with smaller drops.

The shakeout point (your idea, 7 Oct): in the 10 days before the setup the stock dipped under its 20-day low and closed back above it, in the top half of the day, as if taking out stops. It counts only while the market is above its 200-day average: in a falling market a dip under the lows is usually real selling. On the full monthly backtest it lifted 2017–26 from about +752% to +1,207% (typical $10k) and beat the five-point grade in 29 of 30 re-runs that left out a fifth of the stocks, but it was slightly worse in 2020–22 (about 6 points). We took it on the whole history, knowingly bending the every-period rule; the monthly test keeps checking it.
A+ = 4–6 points, A = 3, B = 2, C = 1, D = 0. Avg R a trade by grade (Auto exit): A+ +1.25, A +0.59, B +0.44, C +0.13, D +0.02. The plan, A+ and A, is almost all QM setups (about 150 a year): a Minervini setup only gets there as a Stage 1 or a fast, cheap pullback.
No longer scored (no consistent edge on the honest data): RS 95+, the market regime, the market's 20-day move, base tightness, earnings timing. The old grade took 2 points off for Stage 1, the opposite of what the history shows. ‘Today’ and Account show the old grade alongside while the new one proves itself live.

Pick order: higher score first, then the cheaper stock. It was the only simple rule (with "score, then tightest base") to beat random picking in all four periods. Treat the +970% as the best case: re-run on 30 random 80% slices of the trades it beat random order in 25, by about +67 points over the whole history. ‘Today’ now lists the plan in pick order (Pick #1, #2...). Also tried and rejected as tie-breakers: highest or lowest ADR, deepest pullback, highest RS, least extended, strongest sector.
  • Entries: keep the buy-stop at the pivot. Buying only on a close above the pivot (+207% vs +837% on the same re-simulated trades), a buy-stop a quarter-ADR higher (+314%), skipping the most extended third (+260% vs +770%) and skipping loose bases (+484%) all did worse. Waiting for confirmation costs more in missed and dearer entries than it saves in false breakouts.
  • Sector strength: no edge. Setups in the strongest third of sectors (3-month move) averaged less per trade than the rest in three of four periods. Skipping weak sectors (+405%) or adding a point for strong ones (+247%) both cut the return.

8 · The exit

The stop trails at close − k × ADR once that's above your entry; until then the 1× ADR stop stands. Auto picks k from how far the market was above its 200-day the day before entry: under +5% → 4×, +5–10% → 6×, above → 8×.

9 · Margin (with caution)

With 1.5× buying power the same plan made about 30% a year (worst drop ~27%); at 2×, about 32% (~29%). Bigger drops, and margin interest isn't included. 1.5× is the cautious step; the With margin panel on the Backtest tab keeps the current figures.

10 · Your trades

  • The exits are the gap. On your 16 closed trades graded A+ to B (old grade), selling before the rule made −12.3R; the same trades held to the Auto exit made +8.9R. Over the last 30 days, early exits cost 13.2R on 3 trades. The Account tab now flags each one and keeps a monthly score.
  • The backtest assumes every exit follows the rule. That's the single biggest thing between it and the account.

11 · The checklist

  • Only the plan: A+ and A, or A+ only when ‘Today’ says setups are failing. B and below are off plan.
  • Enter on the buy-stop, not before and not chasing more than ~1% above.
  • Stop at 1× ADR; size so that stop is 0.75% of the account. Same in every market.
  • More setups than cash? Take them in pick order: higher score first, then the cheaper stock.
  • Don't sell a winner by feel. Move the stop to the Auto level each night (the stop email has it) and let it take you out.
  • Check the Discipline panel in Account weekly.

12 · Caveats

  • The first five points were chosen after seeing both halves of the history; their cut-offs come from 2017–22 only and they held on two data sets, but live trading is the real test. The forward test records the new grade and the old one side by side; the Backtest tab's "New grade vs old, live" panel scores them.
  • Delisted and taken-over stocks are missing (no free price history).
  • Results use 0.75% risk and a cash limit on a $10k account; a larger or margin account behaves differently. Many rules were tested to find these; the ones kept held in every period, but live results are the final word.
  • Account results swing a lot on which same-day setups get taken, so read them as ranges, not single numbers.
