Entry-Timing Correction — As-Published vs. Execution-Realistic

The as-published backtest (and the live daily report) both detect a signal using a trading day's OWN closing price, then enter the hypothetical trade AT that same closing price -- a fill only achievable by transacting in the last instant of the session whose data just confirmed the signal. This variant corrects that: every trade instead enters at the next trading session's open, the earliest price a trader reading this report the next morning could actually get filled at. Signal detection, the stop/trail simulation, universe, and date range are all identical between the two runs -- only entry price/timing changed.

$10,000 Portfolio Simulation: Trailing-Stop Multiple Comparison

The flat-$100-per-trade sweep (see the trailing-stop sweep tables in the appendix below) found 8x ADR OR 7% as the per-trade peak (avg R 0.62 across ~20,600 signals). But once trades are run through the same $10k / 1%-compounding / cash-required-is-binding portfolio simulation used everywhere else on this page, that edge does not survive -- a wider trail ties up cash in each position for much longer, which sharply reduces how many trades the account can actually take (capital velocity), and that matters more than the bigger per-trade edge (see the 2x vs 8x comparison in each system's card below). This section re-runs the full $10k simulation once per trailing multiple (1.5x-12x) to find out which multiple genuinely wins once that constraint is applied, instead of assuming the answer.

A++ Setup Analysis: What Do the Biggest & Fastest Winners Have in Common?

Pulled the top 40 closed trades by R-multiple ("grinders" -- the biggest total winners) and, separately, the fastest 40 winners among the 1283 closed trades with R-multiple ≥ 5.0 ("rockets" -- big winners that resolved quickest), then compared their setup-time characteristics against the 20478-trade baseline of every closed trade in the backtest. Everything below is derived from fields already on each trade record plus cached daily OHLC (SMA position, % off the 52-week high, a base-tightness ratio comparing the 10-day range to the 50-day range) -- no new price data downloaded.

Read with caution: no volume data is available in the cached price panels (price_panel_cache.json.gz has no volume field), and true cross-sectional RS rank could not be reconstructed either -- that needs the whole scanned universe's returns on each historical date, not just the traded tickers. Both Minervini's and Qullamaggie's methodologies weight volume heavily (breakout volume surge, VCP volume dry-up), so this analysis is necessarily incomplete on that front. Each group is also only 40 trades out of 20478 -- enough to see a directional lean, not enough to call any of this a hard rule.

Practical checklist

A tight, contracting base sitting within roughly 6-7% of its 52-week high, and not badly extended above the 200-day SMA, favors a multi-month grinder -- the kind of trade that compounds into a huge total winner if held, regardless of which system or pattern flagged it. A name that's already extended well above its moving averages with an elevated ADR%, especially a tight Flag setup or a qullamaggie_momentum signal, favors a fast pop -- resolves in days, not months, but the total R is smaller. Treat this as a lean to weight setups by, not a filter to screen with -- the sample is 40 trades per group, and volume (which both Minervini and Qullamaggie treat as a primary signal) isn't part of this analysis at all.
